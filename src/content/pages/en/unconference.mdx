## What's an unconference?

An unconference is a meetup without a fixed programme. Anyone who turns up can pitch a topic — a question, a problem they're stuck on, something they want to show — and the room picks what to talk about. Sessions are conversations, not talks: whoever pitched it gets it going, and everyone chips in.

## When it works well

- When nobody has a talk ready but people still want to meet
- With a group of 10 to 30 — enough for two or three parallel sessions
- Around a broad theme (AI, careers, side projects) or no theme at all
- As a change of pace between talk-based meetups

## What you need

- One main room, plus one or two corners or side rooms where a small group can talk
- A wall, whiteboard or flipchart for the session board
- Sticky notes and markers — enough for everyone
- A screen in at least one room, for anyone who wants to show something
- A facilitator to run the opening and keep time — that can be you

## How the evening runs

1. 18:00 — Arrive, grab a drink, jot topic ideas on sticky notes
2. 18:15 — Opening: the facilitator explains the format and the ground rules (5 minutes)
3. 18:20 — Pitches: everyone with a topic gets 30 seconds to pitch it
4. 18:35 — Build the board: dot-vote, and the most popular topics get a room and a time slot
5. 18:45 — Two rounds of 30-minute sessions, with a short break in between
6. 19:55 — Closing circle: one sentence per session on what came out of it

## The ground rules

- Whoever comes are the right people
- The law of two feet: if a session isn't working for you, walk to another one — no offence taken
- Whoever pitches a topic kicks it off, but nobody has to present
- When it's over, it's over — sessions can end early

## Tips for the facilitator

- Seed the board with two or three topics of your own so it never starts empty
- Merge overlapping pitches instead of running near-duplicate sessions
- Ask each session to pick someone to jot down notes or links
- Photograph the board and share it, along with the notes, in the community chat
